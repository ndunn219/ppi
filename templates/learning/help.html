How to take this course

What to do first, how each part works, and how your progress is measured.

{% endblock %} {% block content %}

Start here

  1. Set your exam date and weekly hours in Exam settings. The plan assumes about 13 weeks at 8 to 10 hours a week if you have not booked yet.
  2. Take the baseline diagnostic. About two questions per topic plus a short lookup test, timed at exam pace. Don't study for it: it measures where you start.
  3. Accept your plan. My plan orders topics by exam weight times room to grow, so your hours go where the points are, and gives each topic a target week.
  4. Work through topics in plan order using the lesson loop, and do the daily warm-up every day you study.
  5. Follow "Up next" on the Overview. It always names the single most useful thing to do.

The lesson loop

Each topic is taught in the same five steps. Open it from Topics or your plan. You never go more than about 10 minutes without solving something.

{% for key, label, desc in loop %}{% endfor %}
StepWhat happens
{{ forloop.counter }}. {{ label }}{{ desc }}
  • Why a cold try? Attempting a problem before the lesson makes the lesson stick: you know what you're looking for. Getting it wrong is normal and costs nothing.
  • Test out. If the cold try is right, you can test out with two more problems, solved correctly and on pace. Pass both and the topic goes straight to spaced review.
  • Going back. The step pills at the top are links. Open any finished step to look at it again; your progress is kept, and a button takes you back to where you left off. The tutorial can be opened at any time, and each topic page also has a Tutorial button.
  • The tutorial. About 30 minutes: short explanations, explorers you move with sliders, "Try it" questions, and worked examples you reveal one step at a time. Try each step on paper before you reveal it.

Your plan, and falling behind

  • Accepting the plan saves a schedule: each topic you haven't finished gets a target week, based on your exam date and weekly hours. About 70% of your hours go to topics; the rest is for simulations, warm-ups and review.
  • The plan page tells you whether you're on track, and marks topics past their target week as overdue. A topic counts as done when its lesson is finished and it's at Practicing or above.
  • Fell behind? Choose Re-plan from today. It spreads the topics you haven't finished over the weeks you have left.
  • Exam date moved, or your hours changed? Update them on the plan page and the schedule rebuilds from today to fit.
  • Re-planning never erases anything: mastery, answers, flashcards and spaced review stay as they are. And the schedule is a guide: falling behind it never locks you out of anything.

A typical week

For about 9 hours a week, in the Build phase:

  • Every study day (10 to 15 min): the daily warm-up, then due flashcards.
  • Two or three sessions (45 to 90 min): one topic's lesson loop, then a parametric drill on it.
  • Once a week (60 min): a mixed timed block under exam rules.
  • Whenever the error journal fills up: an error replay, plus the fix it suggests for your most common cause of misses.

In Strengthen, shift toward mixed timed blocks and lookup sprints. In Simulate, take two full simulations about a week apart.

How mastery is measured

Each topic has a level, earned only by solving problems with new numbers. Reading, the tutorial and flashcards help you learn but never raise a level by themselves.

LevelWhat it takes
{% level_badge 0 %}No attempts yet.
{% level_badge 1 %}Lesson finished or at least one problem tried.
{% level_badge 2 %}At least half right over your last 4 or more problems.
{% level_badge 3 %}At least 80% right over your last 5, on 2 or more different days, with at most one hint.
{% level_badge 4 %}Proficient, plus on pace (about {{ course.PACE_TARGET_MINUTES|floatformat:0 }} minutes a question), using the right reference, and still right a week or more later.
  • Hints cost half. A problem solved after showing a hint counts as half an attempt.
  • Levels can drop. If a topic's spaced review is overdue, it shows one level lower (in italics) until you review it.
  • "Reference used". Choosing the reference you used on a problem is optional, but it is how the course measures lookup skill for Exam-ready.

Spaced review and flashcards

A topic you get right comes back after 1, 3, 7, 14 and then 30 days, each time with new numbers. A miss sends it back to tomorrow. Cramming doesn't skip ahead: a correct answer only moves a topic along when its review was due.

Flashcards work the same way. After you flip a card, grade yourself honestly: Again if you didn't know it, Hard if it took effort, Good if you knew it, Easy if it was instant. Keyboard: space to flip, then 1 to 4.

Cards ask when a method applies, what its variables mean and where it lives in the references. They never ask you to memorize values the exam supplies.

Fixing your mistakes

  • Every miss gets a cause: concept, method choice, wrong lookup, setup, arithmetic, units or misreading. The wrong answers are built from those mistakes, so the course knows which one you made.
  • "Fix it" appears under each miss with the practice mode for that cause.
  • Error replay brings back each missed problem type, with new numbers, until you solve it twice.
  • Audit your work. Upload a photo of your scratch work and the AI tutor compares it line by line with the verified solution (when the tutor is turned on). Otherwise, use the self-audit checklist, and correct the cause if you think it's wrong.

Exam simulations and references

  • Use an NCEES-approved calculator, and work on a whiteboard or laminated sheet, as on exam day.
  • The real exam gives you the NCEES Handbook and the design standards as searchable PDFs, one open at a time. Here, open your own copy with Open my PDF in the reference panel. It is shown in your browser and never uploaded.
  • The MUTCD is public domain, so the course links to it directly. For other standards it tells you where to look (chapter or part and search terms) but never shows their text.
  • The clock keeps running if you leave the page, and the session submits itself when time is up.

How the content is checked

Every lesson, tutorial, problem type, flashcard and lookup question goes through two kinds of checking.

  • Automatic checks (all content, today). Each problem's answer and wrong choices are computed by code from standard formulas, and tests compare those calculations with hand-worked values. Every tutorial number was recomputed independently, and every diagram is rendered and tested across its full range of inputs.
  • Review by a licensed PE. A licensed Professional Engineer reads each part as you see it and either approves it or asks for changes. Approval confirms the engineering is right, matches current practice and fits how the exam frames questions, which automatic checks can't confirm.

Reports and sharing

Reports has three: readiness, weekly progress, and one for each timed session. Each can be printed or saved as PDF, or shared as a link with an employer, a supervising PE or a study partner.

  • You choose what a shared report includes and how long the link lasts (7, 30 or 90 days).
  • A snapshot link shows the report as it was when you shared it. A live link (readiness only) always shows current data.
  • You can revoke a link at any time, and see how many times it was opened.
  • Reports show only work done in the course, with hints marked.

Questions

Do the questions inside tutorials count?
No. They are for learning. They're saved, but only problems in the lesson loop and practice modes count toward mastery.
Why did a topic's level go down?
Its spaced review is overdue. Do the daily warm-up and it comes back once you answer it.
Can I skip a topic I already know?
Yes: get the cold try right and choose "test out".
